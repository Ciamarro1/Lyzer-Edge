# Reality Evidence Layer (REL) - Evidence Sources Mapping

**Release:** 1.8.5-A
**Domain:** Reality Evidence Layer (REL)
**Authoring Entity:** IEL Team

---

## 1. Evidence Taxonomy

As part of the structural shift from isolated simulations to integrated Reality Approximation Domains (RAD), the system demands a rigorous classification of evidence inputs. All data entering the Reality Evidence Layer (REL) is classified strictly according to the following taxonomy:

*   **Direct Reality Evidence**: Verified, immutable state changes derived from primary reality sources. This includes finalized blockchain state, cryptographically signed execution logs, and irrefutable primary data feeds.
*   **Indirect Evidence**: Corroborative data derived from secondary observations. This includes aggregated market data feeds, verified oracle responses, and observed behavioral metrics that imply state but do not definitively prove it.
*   **Synthetic Evidence**: Data generated strictly within Reality Approximation Domains (RAD) under controlled axioms to mirror potential, future, or hidden reality states. 
*   **Counterfactual Evidence**: Data generated by modeling alternate operational states or divergent outcomes that did not manifest in primary reality but possess a non-zero probability. Used for stress-testing axioms against "what if" scenarios.
*   **Historical Evidence**: Immutable records of past reality states utilized to establish baseline context, backtest axioms, and detect temporal semantic drift.

## 2. Evidence Sources by RCF Shock Type

The Lyzer Resiliency Control Framework (RCF) categorizes operational and environmental perturbations into 5 core Shock Types. The mapping of accepted evidence sources for each shock type is detailed below.

### 2.1 Market & Liquidity Shocks
*   **Direct Reality Evidence**: On-chain DEX liquidity depth, finalized transaction receipts, proven protocol insolvencies.
*   **Indirect Evidence**: Aggregate volume metrics, cross-exchange funding rates, mempool congestion indicators.
*   **Synthetic Evidence**: RAD outputs from simulated hyper-volatility events, cascading liquidation models, and adversarial liquidity drain projections.
*   **Counterfactual Evidence**: Alternative liquidity state models computed under hypothetical flash-crash scenarios that were narrowly avoided.
*   **Historical Evidence**: Data from past extreme volatility events (e.g., historic de-pegs, systemic deleveraging events).

### 2.2 System & Infrastructure Shocks
*   **Direct Reality Evidence**: Primary system uptime logs, cryptographically verified node failures, direct infrastructure state changes.
*   **Indirect Evidence**: Network latency spikes, elevated error rates on peripheral non-critical services, API rate-limit exhaustion patterns.
*   **Synthetic Evidence**: RAD outputs generated from active chaos engineering injections and simulated correlated node outages.
*   **Counterfactual Evidence**: Projected system states assuming the failure of redundant systems during a primary infrastructure compromise.
*   **Historical Evidence**: Post-mortem telemetry logs of historical infrastructure degradations and recovery timelines.

### 2.3 Regulatory & Compliance Shocks
*   **Direct Reality Evidence**: Enacted regulatory mandates, official agency enforcement actions, finalized legal rulings affecting operational domains.
*   **Indirect Evidence**: Draft legislation, public statements from regulatory bodies, shifts in industry legal consensus.
*   **Synthetic Evidence**: RAD modeling of operational constraints under hypothetical draconian regulatory frameworks or immediate jurisdictional bans.
*   **Counterfactual Evidence**: Alternative operational compliance states based on rejected regulatory proposals or alternate legal interpretations.
*   **Historical Evidence**: Documentation of past regulatory shifts and their quantifiable impact on system architecture and market dynamics.

### 2.4 Security & Adversarial Shocks
*   **Direct Reality Evidence**: Confirmed exploit signatures, unauthorized on-chain state transitions, compromised private key signatures, confirmed data breaches.
*   **Indirect Evidence**: Anomalous smart contract interactions, sudden spikes in security honeypot triggers, suspicious but unverified fund movements.
*   **Synthetic Evidence**: RAD outputs from red-team adversarial simulations, penetration testing results, and theoretical zero-day exploit impact models.
*   **Counterfactual Evidence**: Simulated system compromise paths based on patched vulnerabilities, detailing the impact had the patch not been applied.
*   **Historical Evidence**: Threat intelligence reports of past ecosystem hacks, post-mortem exploit vectors, and historical attack signatures.

### 2.5 Macroeconomic & Geopolitical Shocks
*   **Direct Reality Evidence**: Central bank policy rate changes, official macroeconomic data releases (e.g., CPI, NFP), declared geopolitical events.
*   **Indirect Evidence**: Shifts in traditional financial (TradFi) indices, commodity price volatility, global news sentiment indices.
*   **Synthetic Evidence**: RAD modeling of ecosystem impact under extreme stagflation, global conflict scenarios, or sovereign debt crises.
*   **Counterfactual Evidence**: Market behavior modeling under alternate central bank policy decisions (e.g., a rate hike instead of a pause).
*   **Historical Evidence**: Analysis of ecosystem and protocol performance during previous macroeconomic tightening cycles and geopolitical crises.
