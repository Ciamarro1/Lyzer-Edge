# EAR Stability Analysis under Evidence Aging

**Project:** Release 1.8.6-A: Ghost Alpha Investigation Program (GAIP)
**Document Status:** Specification

## Overview

This document analyzes the stability of the Epistemic Alignment Rate (EAR) under the condition of evidence aging. Specifically, it addresses the critical interaction between EAR and the Causal Persistence Score (CPS) to detect underlying structural decay in analytical models.

## Integration of Causal Persistence Score (CPS)

While EAR measures the immediate alignment of our epistemic models with observable data, it does not inherently account for the temporal degradation of the underlying causal structures that produced the data. To accurately gauge the robustness of our interpretations as evidence ages, we must integrate the Causal Persistence Score (CPS). CPS quantifies the ongoing validity of the causal mechanisms driving the phenomena under observation.

## The Signature of Silent Causal Death

The most critical vulnerability in systems relying solely on EAR is the phenomenon of **silent causal death**. The primary signature for this condition is strictly defined as:

**`EAR High + CPS Falling`**

### Understanding the Divergence

When we observe a high EAR alongside a falling CPS, a dangerous structural divergence is occurring. This state indicates that:

1. **The Interpretation Appears Correct (High EAR):** Our models are still successfully predicting or aligning with surface-level observables. The historical momentum of the system means that superficial patterns and correlations have not yet visibly broken down.
2. **The Underlying Causality is Dead (Falling CPS):** The actual causal drivers that initially generated those patterns have structurally degraded, shifted, or ceased to exist. 

In essence, this divergence means that while the surface interpretation appears robust, the foundational logic of the system is no longer intact. We are epistemically aligned with a "ghost" of the past data-generating process rather than the current reality. Failing to detect this divergence—by looking only at EAR—leads to a sudden, catastrophic collapse in predictive accuracy once the residual momentum of the surface observables finally dissipates.
