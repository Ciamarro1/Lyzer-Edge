# EML Unknown Registry
## Worker EML-E | Release 1.8.8

This registry serves as a constitutional amendment by the CIA (Central Intelligence Architecture) for the Empirical Memory Layer. It documents the epistemological boundaries of our current understanding, rigorously defining what we do not know, what we might not know we do not know, and what we are explicitly forbidden from inferring based on current empirical data.

### 1. Known Unknowns
*Areas where we are aware of a lack of knowledge or data.*

*   **Real EAR (Expected Agent Reward) Distribution:** We do not currently have sufficient long-term, statistically significant data to definitively plot the true probability distribution of EAR across diverse market conditions.
*   **System Degradation Rates under Sustained Volatility:** The exact rate and specific failure modes of architectural components under continuous, high-stress market volatility over extended periods remain unquantified.
*   **Edge-Case Market Microstructure Impact:** How unprecedented microstructural anomalies (e.g., flash crashes of novel asset classes) will interact with our current execution logic is theoretically modeled but empirically unverified.
*   **True Latency Floor in Production:** While theoretical lower bounds are established, the absolute minimum achievable latency under full production load with all security and logging overheads is not yet fully profiled.

### 2. Unknown Unknown Candidates
*Hypotheses regarding potential blind spots or systemic risks we have not yet formalized.*

*   **Emergent Agent Collusion:** The possibility that isolated subagents might develop unintended, implicitly collusive behaviors through long-term interaction with the same shared environment or data streams, leading to systemic bias.
*   **Unmodeled Macro-Economic Phase Shifts:** Structural shifts in global markets that our current historical datasets do not encompass (e.g., a fundamentally new monetary regime), rendering existing predictive models invalid in ways we cannot predict.
*   **Hardware-Level Exploits in Cloud Infrastructure:** Unknown vulnerabilities in the underlying physical hardware of our cloud providers that could subtly alter computation or timing in ways our software monitors cannot detect.
*   **Semantic Drift in External Data Feeds:** Gradual, undocumented changes in how third-party data providers calculate or report metrics, leading to an unnoticed divergence between our models and reality.

### 3. Forbidden Inferences
*Conclusions that are strictly prohibited due to insufficient sample size, incomplete data, or epistemological constraints.*

*   **"The Architecture Works"**: This is a terminal state assertion. We can only infer that the architecture *has not failed yet* under the *observed conditions*.
*   **"EAR is High"**: EAR cannot be deemed "high" or "low" absolutely; it can only be evaluated relative to a specific risk profile and market regime over a statistically significant horizon, which we currently lack.
*   **"The Model is Generalized"**: Performance on historical backtests and limited forward-testing does not constitute proof of generalization. True generalization is a hypothesis, not a proven fact.
*   **"Risk is Fully Contained"**: We can assert that *known risks* are mitigated according to *current models*, but absolute risk containment is an invalid inference in any complex, non-deterministic system.
